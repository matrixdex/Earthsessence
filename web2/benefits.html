  2. Anti-inflammatory

    Thymoquinone reduces inflammation and is studied for inflammatory conditions like rheumatoid arthritis and fever.

    Source: PubMed Central ↗

  3. Anticancer

    Thymoquinone stops the cell cycle and causes apoptosis cell death in many types of cancer cells. Animal studies demonstrate this.

    Source: Nature Index ↗

  4. Antimicrobial and antifungal

    Thymoquinone acts against certain bacteria and fungi to fight infections.

    Source: PubMed Central ↗

Honey

Honey is a natural food with many active compounds.

    Honey contains phenolic acids, flavonoids and enzymes. These compounds protect cells from oxidative stress.

    Source: PubMed Central ↗

  2. Cough relief

    Clinical trials show honey reduces cough in children.

    Source: PubMed Central ↗

  3. Wound healing

    Honey acts against bacteria to heal wounds.

    Source: PubMed Central ↗

  4. Digestive support

    Honey contains prebiotic oligosaccharides. These feed good gut bacteria: Lactobacillus and Bifidobacteria.

    Source: Frontiers in Nutrition ↗

    Honey intake results in higher sperm count, better sperm movement, and higher testosterone in men with fertility problems.

    Source: Journal study ↗
